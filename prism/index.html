PRISM: Prior-informed Robotic Imitation Learning with Structured Motion Augmentation For Geometrically Constrained Manipulation

Illustration of geometrically constrained tasks. Unlike simple grasping, these tasks require the robot to control its motion along a specific path, such as painting or polishing.

📄 Abstract

Learning robust robot manipulation policies from limited demonstrations remains a significant challenge, particularly for geometrically constrained tasks that require maintaining consistent spatial relationships. While data augmentation techniques have shown promise in improving policy generalization, the acquisition of diverse, high-quality datasets remains a primary bottleneck in data-driven robotics. In this paper, we present PRISM (Prior-informed robotic imitation learning with structured motion augmentation), a lightweight framework that systematically expands a single demonstration into a diverse and feasible dataset for geometrically constrained manipulation tasks. We use motion capture to acquire kinematic priors, apply saliency-guided kinematic refinement to remove redundant motion phases, and generate diverse trajectory variants that preserve geometric relationships. We evaluate PRISM in multiple manipulation scenarios and demonstrate that it achieves near-instantaneous prototype trajectory acquisition and efficient scalability to large datasets, while maintaining competitive policy performance. These results confirm that PRISM is a practical solution for single-demonstration robot learning tasks where rapid data generation and geometric constraint preservation are prioritized. We present a kinematic-prior-driven data generation paradigm that directly operates within the feasible constraint space of tasks, ensuring both physical plausibility and scalability.

🔄 2. Structured Augmentation Mechanism

We propose a structured mechanism that transforms a single demonstration into a diverse dataset by aligning the task space and applying transformations to preserve geometric consistency.

📊 3. Extensive Validation

We validate our approach through extensive experiments on multiple robotic geometric tasks to demonstrate enhanced data efficiency and generalization compared to other methods.

⚙️ Method Overview

Overview of the PRISM framework showing the pipeline from motion capture to policy training.

PRISM consists of three main components:

  1. Kinematic Prior Acquisition: Use motion capture to directly record high-precision demonstrations, capturing the continuous and nuanced motion in real-time.
  2. Saliency-Guided Refinement: Apply keyframe selection to compress dense trajectories into compact kinematic priors by retaining dynamically and geometrically informative frames.
  3. Structured Augmentation: Generate diverse trajectory variants through similarity group transformations (Sim(3)) that preserve geometric relationships while exploring the feasible set.

💡 Conclusion

We introduced PRISM, a lightweight framework to learn geometrically constrained manipulation policies from a single demonstration. PRISM combines motion-capture-based kinematic priors, saliency-guided kinematic redundancy refinement, and structured trajectory augmentation to preserve task-consistent geometry while efficiently covering the feasible set. Extensive experiments in multiple manipulation scenarios showed that PRISM achieves near-instantaneous acquisition of a prototype trajectory and scales efficiently to datasets, substantially reducing the cost of data generation compared to Teleop and MP. In addition, policies trained on PRISM-generated data achieved competitive success rates across tasks.
